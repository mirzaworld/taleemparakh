Scholarship fee scams: how to spot a fake award

Genuine scholarships do not charge you to apply or to 'release' an award. Apply only on the scholarship's own official website, and ignore WhatsApp groups and forwarded letters. If a message asks for a processing, registration, release or visa fee, treat it as a scam sign and do not pay.

What the scam looks like

You may get a message, a call or a letter saying you were selected for a scholarship you never applied for, or that your application is accepted. Then you are asked to pay a small or large fee to 'process', 'register' or 'release' the award, or for 'visa processing'.

The US Federal Trade Commission gives the rule in plain words: never pay to apply for a scholarship. It lists 'you just pay a processing fee' as a scam line. If someone asks for a processing cost, walk away.

In Roman Urdu: Scholarship ke naam par fees maangi jaye to paise na dein, aur pehle us scholarship ki apni website par check karein.

Example of wording to watch for

'Congratulations, you are selected. Pay the visa processing fee to release your award.' In Roman Urdu: 'scholarship confirm hai, bas processing fee jama karwa dein'. These are examples of the style, not quotes from one case.

A real case in Pakistan

The state news agency APP reported in July 2026 on a case registered by the FIA. A consultancy was accused of selling 'HEC-funded PhD scholarships' together with admission to a US university for 10,000 US dollars from each person, using forged documents. APP says HEC checked the documents and declared them fake.

We describe the method only. If you or your family paid in a case like this, you did not do anything wrong, and you can report it. See 'I already paid a scammer or fake agent: what to do now'.

Signs to watch for

  1. A fee to apply, process, register or release a scholarship.
  2. A message or call that says you won something you did not apply for.
  3. Pressure to pay today, or to keep it secret. Chevening says only criminals will try to rush or panic you.
  4. A WhatsApp group or a link that collects your documents or money. Scholarship bodies use their own websites.
  5. Someone claiming to be a government office, such as the Home Office or UK Visas and Immigration (UKVI), who contacts you about a scholarship payment.

How to apply safely

  1. Search for the scholarship's name, find its own official website, and type the address yourself.
  2. Apply only through that website. Never send your passport, bank details or one-time codes through a chat.
  3. Ask the scholarship body in writing, using the contact on its own website, whether it contacted you.
  4. Talk to a parent, teacher or counsellor before you pay or sell anything.

What TaleemParakh could not confirm

We did not find statements on the websites of every major scholarship body saying that it never charges fees, so we do not quote them here. The safest rule is the same for all: you never pay to apply for or to receive a scholarship. Check any claim on that scholarship's own website.

Questions people ask

Do real scholarships ever charge a fee to release the money?

The US Federal Trade Commission says never pay to apply for a scholarship. A 'processing' or 'release' fee is a scam sign.

Is a scholarship WhatsApp group an official route?

No. Apply only on the scholarship body's own official website, and ignore groups and forwarded letters.
