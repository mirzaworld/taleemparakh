Warning signs of a fake study visa agent or consultant

Walk away from anyone who guarantees a visa or admission, asks you to keep it secret, wants a large cash payment, or keeps your passport or login. We found no government licence for study-abroad consultants in Pakistan, so a 'licence' claim needs checking. Ask the university whether the agent represents it, and keep control of your own portal and visa account.

Warning signs

The Australian High Commission in Pakistan says fake agents are targeting Pakistani migrants. Its warning signs are listed below. Not every agent who does one of these is a scammer, but each one is a reason to stop and check.

You are not at fault if you were approached. These people are practised and persuasive.

  1. They contact you through WhatsApp or Facebook Messenger.
  2. They 'guarantee' a visa or admission. For example, in Roman Urdu: 'visa pakka hai' (our example, not the High Commission's wording).
  3. They ask for large upfront payments, or charge more than the fee published by the official body.
  4. They pressure you to act quickly. For example, in Roman Urdu: 'aaj hi payment karein, seats khatam ho rahi hain' (our example, not the High Commission's wording).
  5. They ask you to hand over your passport.
  6. They refuse to share your application or login, or tell you not to discuss it with other people. For example, in Roman Urdu: 'kisi ko mat batana' (our example, not the High Commission's wording).
  7. They say you can come on one visa and 'switch' to another after you arrive.

Stop if you see any of these

Do not pay. Paste the message into the Message check, show it to a parent or teacher, and ask the university directly.

Is there a licence to check?

We found no government licence or public register for study-abroad consultants in Pakistan. The main association of education consultants says on its own website that the government has not yet regulated this profession, and that it has run its own registration of consultants since 2014.

The Bureau of Emigration and Overseas Employment licenses Overseas Employment Promoters. Its website is about jobs abroad. An overseas employment licence is not a licence to send students to study.

So if a consultant says 'we are government licensed', ask which body issued the licence, for which service, and open that body's own website to check. A business card, an office or a company name is not authorisation by a university.

What a British Council badge means

The British Council says it does not formally endorse, accredit or validate agents and counsellors. It awards a digital certificate and badge for training, valid for two years. A badge shows training. It does not show that a university or the government has authorised the agent.

What you can check yourself

  1. Ask the university in writing whether the agent represents it, using the email address on the university's own website.
  2. For Australian visa help, check the person on the register of migration agents. The Australian Government says paid immigration help is illegal unless the person is a registered migration agent or an Australian legal practitioner.
  3. Ask for a written contract and a receipt for every payment, in your own name.
  4. Keep your own passport, your own portal login and your own visa account. A fair agent helps you without taking control.
  5. Never give your password or one-time codes to anyone.

Paid 'agents' who check documents

The German Embassy in Islamabad has warned about so-called agents who offered to check applications and demanded very high prices. It says the German missions are not related to these agents in any way, and that their service may be without any value and in no case binds the German consular sections. The same idea applies to any country: an agent cannot promise what an embassy will decide.

Questions people ask

Is there an official licence for education consultants in Pakistan?

We found none. The consultants' association says the profession is not yet regulated. Licences from the Bureau of Emigration are for overseas jobs.

Does a British Council certificate mean the agent is approved?

No. The British Council says it does not endorse, accredit or validate agents. The badge shows they completed training.
