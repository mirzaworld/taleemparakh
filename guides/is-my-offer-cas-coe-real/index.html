Is my offer letter, CAS (Confirmation of Acceptance for Studies) or CoE (Confirmation of Enrolment) real?

No website, including this one, can confirm your own offer, CAS or CoE: only the university can. Log in to its applicant portal by typing the address of its website yourself, then email the admissions office at the address published there and ask it to confirm. A deposit before a CAS or CoE is normal, but you pay it only to the university.

Why a document can look perfect and still be fake

Software and printers can copy any letter. A fake can have the right logo, the right words and a real-looking number. That is why you check the source, not the paper.

Fake documents have been sold at scale. In one UK case, over 1,300 fake CAS were sold. In Canada, fake letters of acceptance came from unauthorised consultants. If you are worried, you have not done anything wrong. You are doing the right thing by checking.

The safe check, step by step

  1. Find the university's website yourself, by typing it or searching for it. Do not use a link from the agent.
  2. Find the applicant portal from that website and log in with your own account. Your offer or CAS should be visible there.
  3. Find the admissions or international office email on the same website. Write from your own email and ask them to confirm your offer, the course and the fee in your name.
  4. If you cannot log in, or the portal shows nothing, ask the admissions office. Do not pay anyone until you have a reply from the university itself.
  5. Never give your portal password or one-time codes to an agent.

Deposits before a CAS or CoE are normal

Many universities ask for a deposit first. For example, one UK university states a standard overseas tuition deposit of 4,000 pounds, and another states 100 percent of tuition for postgraduate applicants from Pakistan before the CAS can be issued. Study Australia says the CoE is sent after you accept the Letter of Offer and pay your deposit.

A deposit is not the warning sign. The warning sign is paying a person, an agent or a different account. Pay only through the payment page linked from the university's own website. See 'How to pay university fees and rent safely'.

Check the payee

If the account name is a person, or the payment details came by WhatsApp from an agent, stop and ask the university which account it uses.

Each country has its own safeguards

United Kingdom: a CAS is a record in the university's own system. Check it with the university. The GOV.UK site says the Home Office will never contact you to ask for money. Its official email addresses are in the form name.surname@homeoffice.gov.uk. An email can show an address like this and still be fake, so contact the Home Office through GOV.UK yourself.

Australia: ask the provider to confirm your CoE, and check the provider and course on the official CRICOS (the Commonwealth Register of Institutions and Courses for Overseas Students) register.

United States: only the school's Designated School Official issues an I-20. Recruiters have no role in issuing or handling it.

Canada: from 1 December 2023 the school must confirm every post-secondary letter of acceptance directly with the immigration department, so ask the school to confirm yours.

If the university has withdrawn your CAS

The PIE News reported in May 2026 that UK Visas and Immigration does not require universities to tell students before they withdraw a CAS. That can be a shock. The way to solve it is to write to the university, not to pay an agent or someone who rings you.

A fake letter claiming to be from UK Visas and Immigration (UKVI) circulated in Pakistan in 2024. The British High Commission told people: delete it and do not share it. Check any such claim on GOV.UK.

Questions people ask

Can I check a CAS number online?

The reliable check is with the university, not on a CAS checker website. A CAS is a record in the university's own system, so log in to the university's portal and ask the admissions office to confirm it. Be careful with any website that offers to check a CAS number for you.

My agent says the university portal is not needed. Is that normal?

No. Keep control of your own login. An agent who says you do not need to see your own application is a warning sign.
